Glyphs 4 script · Extremes: Balanced

Nodes on extremes.
Curves that behave.

Most tools stop once the extremes have nodes. This one keeps going: it removes the diagonal nodes, balances every handle pair and evens out curvature across each node, in every master at once, and stays as close to your drawing as you ask.

The problem

Adding extremes is the easy half.

Put nodes on the extremes and you are left with three things to fix by hand, in every master: the old diagonal nodes, handles that grew long and lopsided when those nodes went, and curvature that jumps at every node. You can see the jumps in a curvature comb as steps and gaps.

The orange lines are a curvature comb, as in SpeedPunk. The longer the spike, the tighter the curve. A smooth comb means a smooth curve. A step in the comb at a node means the curvature jumps there.

What it does, in order

Five steps, one button.

  1. Split at the extremes, exactly

    Every curve whose horizontal or vertical extreme falls inside it is cut there with de Casteljau. Before rounding, the shape is identical.

  2. Remove the diagonal nodes

    Smooth nodes that are not on an extreme go, in all masters together so they stay compatible. The Closeness slider decides how far that may move the outline.

  3. Balance the handles

    Both handles of a curve end at the same share of the way to the point where their directions cross (the Tunni point), and neither passes it, so no curve bulges past its own arc. This is the Tunnify measure.

  4. Harmonize the curvature

    At each extreme node the curvature is matched on both sides (G2). Green Harmony slides the node along its own handle line, so it stays on the extreme, then the handles are refitted. Half steps repeat while the slider allows.

  5. Round, check, write

    All coordinates become whole units. Extremes left inside segments and fractional points are checked, a backup layer is saved, and the result is written. Cmd+Z undoes it.

Balance: plain refit and balanced

No more handles reaching past the arc.

Fitting curves to the old outline without a balance rule gives the shape back, but not good handles. In the P bowl the lower handle runs almost down to the stem junction. Balanced, both handles stop at the same share of the way to the Tunni point.

Closeness: 1:1 and Loose

You choose how much the drawing may move.

1:1 keeps every node where it is and only adds extremes, so the outline stays within half a unit. The price is extra nodes and curvature that cannot be evened out. Loose lets the extremes slide and the curves settle, giving fewer nodes and a smooth comb, and the outline moves a little more. In between are Almost 1:1 and Close, and By eye at the far end.

Why it saves time

A glyph's worth of fiddling, in one pass.

By hand, per glyph

  • Add Extremes, then hunt down the diagonal nodes and delete them one by one.
  • Drag handles until the shape comes back, with nothing to tell you when they are balanced.
  • Open a curvature view, nudge nodes until the comb stops stepping, then check the shape again.
  • Repeat in every master and keep the node structure compatible yourself.
  • Round coordinates afterwards and hope nothing kinked.

With Extremes: Balanced

  • One button. All masters at once, or the current one only.
  • Diagonal nodes removed, extremes placed, handles balanced, curvature harmonized.
  • Whole units, no new kinks, nothing past a Tunni point.
  • A backup layer per master and plain Cmd+Z.
  • A report with how far the outline moved, the handle imbalance and the curvature jump.
